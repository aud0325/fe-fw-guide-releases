    Part III Dagsion RNG observations

    A community experiment in Part III uses Eshmel’s trips into and out of Dagsion to change world-map outcomes. Separate procedures explain enemy placement, gathering retries and saving before a lure completes, including when autosaves change.

    In this guide

    2. When an enemy blocks your route · Spoilers
    3. Retrying a gathering outcome · Spoilers

    What the experiment changes · Spoilers
    i

    On the Part III world map, Eshmel remains at Dagsion while other companions explore in multiple parties. The author used his ability to enter and leave Dagsion without spending a turn, reporting changes to enemy placement, gathering outcomes and animals produced by lures after these trips.

    The procedure changes what happens before an outcome is resolved, rather than simply reloading the same save. In the gathering example, reloading the save made just after leaving Dagsion and gathering again produced the same result, so each retry included another trip into and out of the city.

    These are observations from an individual experiment, not a guarantee that every random outcome or desired reward can be controlled. The procedure creates new autosaves. Before starting, decide whether you can afford to replace the autosave you might otherwise want to return to.

    When an enemy blocks your route · Spoilers
    i

    In the author’s example, an enemy blocking the intended route disappeared after the trip, avoiding a detour and wasted turns. The source does not report defeating it or receiving combat rewards; use this to recheck the route.

    1. Check the exploring party’s intended route and the enemy blocking it.
    2. Switch to Eshmel at Dagsion, enter the city, then return to the world map. The source notes that L/R cycles between party locations, including Eshmel’s position.
    3. Return to the exploring party and inspect enemy placement before moving. Check whether the route has actually opened; the example does not establish that the enemy disappears on every attempt.
    Retrying a gathering outcome · Spoilers
    i

    The key is returning to a state before gathering, rather than a save made after it. The source uses the autosave created on leaving Dagsion, while gathering is still available. The author presents an example in which a BAD result changed to GOOD on a retry.

    1. Save before the gathering action you want to attempt. Do not gather yet.
    2. Use Eshmel to enter and leave Dagsion. The source reports that this changes world-map outcomes and creates an autosave while gathering is still unperformed.
    3. Gather with the exploring party and inspect the result. Continue normally if you accept it.
    4. To retry, return to the title screen and load the autosave made just after leaving Dagsion. Check that the gathering action is still unperformed in the resumed state.
    5. Do not immediately repeat the same gathering action. Enter Dagsion again, leave for the world map, then gather. Compare the result after this new trip and its new autosave.
    Comparing animals produced by a lure · Spoilers
    i

    A lure produces an animal on the world map when its counter completes. The author saved at counter 4, entered and left Dagsion, then moved one space to inspect the resulting animal. That counter value and single-space movement describe this experiment, not a universal setup for every lure.

    Across five attempts including that movement, the author reported changes to the horse’s appearance or skills, and a different kind of horse on the fifth attempt. This is distinct from repeatedly visiting Dagsion beside an animal that has already appeared: the experiment returned to a state before the final action that triggered its appearance.

    1. To compare the same setup, save while the lure counter is at 4. Keep this distinct from a save made after the animal has already appeared.
    2. Enter and leave Dagsion with Eshmel, then move the exploring party one space and inspect the result.
    3. For another comparison, reload the state before the final movement, make another trip into and out of Dagsion, then repeat the same one-space movement. The author avoided adding other actions when comparing outcomes.
    Limits when using the method · Spoilers
    i
    • The action reported to cost no turns is Eshmel’s trip into and out of Dagsion. The lure experiment also includes movement; it does not establish that moving or other actions are free.
    • The source provides gathering and enemy-placement examples plus five lure comparisons, but no version-by-version testing or probability of obtaining a specific material or mount. A given number of trips cannot guarantee a particular result.
    • The suggestion that gathering could replace the final movement in the lure procedure is the author’s speculation. The steps above retain the movement used in the reported comparison.
    • If the loaded save is already after gathering or the animal’s appearance, it is not the same setup as the source. Further trips do not restore an older autosave, so check the save’s point in the sequence before deciding whether to continue.
